# KnowMe — Hackathon Submission

## Pitch (one paragraph)

KnowMe is a private, calm journal for people changing a habit — cannabis, nicotine, alcohol, or their own. In ten seconds you log the moment a craving shows up: how strong it was, where you were, how you felt, whether you rode it out, and what helped. KnowMe keeps everything on your phone, shows you your own patterns in plain language ("Most of your cravings land between 7 and 10pm"), and, only when you tap **Reflect**, asks Meta Llama to mirror your week back to you in four to six sentences ending with one open question. In a hard moment, **I'm in it** shows you your own reasons, your top helpers, and a slow breath circle. It never tells you what to do, never shames you, and never calls anything a relapse or a failure. Know thyself — the data is yours, the conclusions are yours.

## Problem

People trying to change a habit are usually offered two bad options: streak apps that punish a slip with red failure states and broken counters, or generic advice that ignores when, where, and why *their* cravings actually happen. The moment that matters — mid-craving, often in the evening — is exactly when a lecture, a label, or a login screen is least useful. KnowMe starts from the opposite premise: a person who can see their own patterns clearly, in their own words, doesn't need to be managed. They need a mirror that is fast enough to use in the moment (ten seconds, one thumb), honest enough to trust (no orders, no diagnosis), and private enough to be truthful in.

## How Llama is used — and why only aggregates are sent

Meta Llama powers one feature: **Reflect**, a short weekly letter. When — and only when — the user taps Reflect, the app builds a `ReflectionSummary` on-device (`buildSummary` in `src/features/reflect/logic.ts`) and sends that summary, through a small Vercel proxy (`proxy/api/reflect.ts`) that holds the API key off the phone, to Llama (currently `meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo` on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint; model and base URL are env vars). The system prompt constrains the model to 4–6 short, calm sentences in the user's own words, no advice, no medical guidance, no "relapse / addict / failure," ending with exactly one open question.

Only aggregates leave the device because raw journal entries are the most sensitive data in the app, and Llama doesn't need them to notice a pattern. The summary contains counts (moments, rode-it-out vs. used), average intensity, a 24-bucket count by hour of day, the top three contexts, feelings, and helpers, plus the habit names and the user's own reasons — written by them to be shown back. No timestamps for individual moments, no device identity beyond a random per-install ID used only for rate limiting, and **no free-text notes unless the user turns on "Include my notes" for that one reflection**. Everything else — the SQLite database, the charts in Patterns, the crisis check — is computed locally and works offline. Sending less is also what makes the safety checks tractable: a small, structured summary can be screened reliably before and after the model call, which raw diary text could not.

## Privacy approach

Private by default is a structural property, not a policy page. All habits, reasons, and moments live in on-device SQLite (`expo-sqlite`); there are no accounts, no cloud sync, no analytics SDKs, and no crash reporters that send content. The only network call in the app is the opt-in Reflect request above, to our own proxy. The proxy logs token counts, error codes, and *which* crisis check flagged (`keyword`, `screen`, or `output`) — never the summary text, never the model output, never the install ID. Crisis text is not stored anywhere in the system. Demo data is isolated with an `is_demo` flag so a demonstration never mixes with a real journal. The Me tab states this in plain language and keeps **Get support** (988, SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357, 1-800-QUIT-NOW, and "tell someone you trust") one tap away at all times.

## Safety design

Safety overrides every other feature (`SAFETY.md`), and it is layered so no single model call is trusted. **1) Local first:** every free-text field runs an on-device keyword check (`src/safety/crisisTerms.ts`) before anything is saved or sent; a match immediately shows a full-screen card — call or text **988**, **911** for emergency, "You don't have to figure this out alone" — never delayed behind a network call, and the moment is not saved. **2) Proxy input checks:** the proxy re-runs the keyword list, then a focused Llama screen (a YES/NO crisis classification) before generating anything; if the screen cannot run, it fails closed and no reflection is produced. **3) Output check:** a `CRISIS` token or crisis language in the model's reply is replaced with `{ crisis: true }`, and the app shows the same card instead of the letter. The system prompt additionally forbids medical and withdrawal advice (including answering "how much can I safely use?" — it redirects to a doctor), dosing or moderation suggestions, diagnosis and shaming labels, and claiming to be a therapist or human. The letter is labeled in the UI: "Written by Llama from a private summary." These paths are covered by on-device tests, an app/proxy term-list parity test, and a crisis eval harness; we are explicit that the eval is ~70 self-written English phrases, not a clinical validation — that is top of the post-hackathon list.

## What's next: GuideMe

Today KnowMe deliberately has no coach — reflecting beats prescribing. The next step, already named out-of-scope in `PRODUCT.md`, is **GuideMe**: an in-the-moment coach for the minutes inside a craving, not a general chatbot. When a user taps **I'm in it**, GuideMe would sit alongside their reasons, top helpers, and the breath timer and offer short, grounded companionship — noticing what has helped *this* person before ("Last time at this hour, a walk helped") and asking, never ordering. It would run under the same constraints that make Reflect trustworthy: the same aggregate-only context (no raw-note firehose), the same local-first crisis card ahead of any model call, the same banned-language and no-medical-advice rules, on-device crisis screening of both sides of the conversation, and a visible "this is Llama, not a person" label. Before GuideMe ships to anyone real: a larger, professionally reviewed crisis eval (more phrasings, more languages), a shared rate limiter, and device checks at the largest Dynamic Type sizes — all tracked in `TODO.md`. The principle doesn't change: people over the app, reflection before advice, and the calmest screen in the room.
